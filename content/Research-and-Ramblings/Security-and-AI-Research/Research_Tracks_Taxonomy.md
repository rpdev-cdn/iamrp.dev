# 26 Prioritized Research Tracks Taxonomy

## Architectural Problem Statement

Security research areas overlap: agent security, detection engineering, infrastructure, privacy, and incident response all depend on shared data and controls. A flat list of tracks can hide those relationships, encourage duplicate effort, and make it difficult to compare topics or direct finite research capacity toward the most consequential gaps.

The taxonomy organizes prioritized tracks as a navigable map rather than a claim that one fixed ranking fits every organization. Its value depends on transparent definitions, links between foundational and applied topics, and a process for updating priority as threats, systems, and evidence evolve.

## Core Design & Implementation

- Define each track with a consistent record: problem domain, motivating risk, key questions, relevant assets or stakeholders, expected methods, and dependencies. Use shared terminology so similar tracks can be compared without erasing their differences.
- Represent relationships among tracks, such as prerequisite knowledge, common datasets, enabling controls, and downstream operational use. This exposes opportunities for coordinated work and dependencies that could otherwise delay a research program.
- Prioritize with explicit factors including risk relevance, uncertainty, expected decision value, feasibility, and overlap with current capabilities. Treat scores as aids to discussion, not objective measurements of value.
- Review the taxonomy periodically and preserve rationale for changes. Archive completed or superseded tracks with links to outcomes, distinguish active work from candidate topics, and identify gaps where no current track addresses an important need.
