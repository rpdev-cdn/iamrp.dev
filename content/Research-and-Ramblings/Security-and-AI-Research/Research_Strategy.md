# Security Engineering & Research Strategy

## Architectural Problem Statement

Security research competes for finite time, infrastructure, and specialist attention, while new vulnerabilities and technology shifts continuously change the landscape. A collection of interesting topics is not yet a strategy: without explicit goals, selection criteria, and a route from exploration to validation, work can fragment or produce findings that never inform defensive decisions.

An effective research strategy links mission needs to questions, methods, evidence, and decisions. It should balance immediate operational risk with foundational investigation, while being honest about uncertainty, ethical boundaries, and the difference between a promising hypothesis and a validated result.

Stakeholders need to understand why work was selected and what evidence would justify changing direction or moving a result into practice.

## Core Design & Implementation

- Translate organizational risks into research questions with defined scope, intended decision, and success criteria. Prioritize using impact, likelihood, uncertainty, dependency, and feasibility rather than novelty alone.
- Match methods to questions: literature and standards review, controlled experiments, telemetry analysis, threat modeling, or adversary emulation. Record assumptions, data provenance, constraints, and authorization for each study.
- Maintain a portfolio with owners, milestones, dependencies, and review points. Time-box exploratory work, communicate negative or inconclusive results, and provide a clear path for promising results to enter engineering, detection, or policy workflows.
- Reassess priorities as evidence and conditions change. Apply privacy, safety, and responsible disclosure requirements, protect sensitive datasets, and track follow-up actions so research produces measurable operational learning without overstating impact.
