Infectious Disease/Pandemic Policy
                                      


Approval Date               7/15/11

Approved By                 John D. Clunk, President




 will take proactive steps to protect the workplace in the event of an
infectious disease outbreak, threat of influenza or other pandemic that causes serious widespread
illness. First and foremost, we want to maintain a safe workplace and encourage and/or adopt
practices protecting the health of employees, clients, visitors or others. We also want to ensure
the continuity of business operations in the event of a severe influenza pandemic. The policies
described below are intended to achieve these objectives. Our efforts will be guided by and in
accordance with all applicable federal, state and local laws. We will monitor information
provided by the World Medical Association and advise on the issue and modify or supplement
this policy as necessary.

1. Preventing the Spread of the Flu in the Workplace
Employees are encouraged to take steps to reduce the transmission of influenza or other
infectious disease in the workplace.

By all reports, the best strategy for reducing the transmission of influenza remains the most
obvious,
      Cover your mouth and nose with a tissue when you cough or sneeze.
      Put your used tissue in the waste basket.
      If you don’t have a tissue, cough or sneeze into your upper sleeve or elbow, not your
       hands.
      Wash your hands often with soap and warm water for 20 seconds.
      If soap and water are not available, use an alcohol-based hand rub.
We encourage all employees to get an influenza vaccination on an annual basis prior to the start
of the flu season. We have alcohol-based hand sanitizers throughout the workplace and in
common areas.

2. General Guidelines
Management will monitor and coordinate the appropriate actions necessary during a threat of
influenza or other pandemic by addressing the following issues:
      Paid Time Off (PTO) is provided in an effort to make all reasonable attempts to ensure
       that employees not attend the workplace while displaying symptoms of illness or if
       subject to quarantine directives. If PTO is exhausted and an employee is still displaying
       symptoms of illness or under quarantine, the employee will not be paid but will not be
       disciplined for missing work due to illness.
      Creating a culture of infection control in the workplace that is reinforced during the
       annual influenza season, to include, worker education, and if possible, options for
       working offsite while ill, cancellation of scheduled vacations, approval of overtime, re-
       assignment of staff to different departments, re-assignment of work within a department
       and additional use of part-time staff.
      Establishing contingency plans to maintain delivery of services during times of
       significant and sustained worker absenteeism.
Management also appoints department heads and other appropriate staff to assist the
coordination of monitoring for signs of a pandemic situation. Each department head will have a
back-up to assume their duties in case of their own illness. This person will be kept current on
all emergency procedures and this list will be kept with this plan and updated as needed.


It is the duty of Management to:
      Monitor issues and information related to pandemics to keep this plan up to date.
      Recommend any changes to the plan as circumstances warrant.
      Communicate with public health agencies, emergency responders and others, if the
       circumstances warrant should an outbreak occur.
      Implement this plan should it become necessary.

Department Heads will have the following responsibilities:
      Identify and communicate to Management which employees and systems are essential to
       maintaining operations.
      Identify and communicate to Management the names of possible ancillary employees
       who can perform certain job duties in the case of a pandemic.
      Develop and communicate to Management an emergency communications plan for their
       department notifying key personnel and clients.
      Develop and communicate to Management a plan to continue operations in each
       department with the least possible number of staff.
      Ensure that all employees in their departments are adequately trained on emergency
       procedures in the case of a pandemic and in the prevention of illness.
      Encourage all employees to participate in the annual influenza vaccination program
       sponsored by the law firm.


3. Activation Plan
Loss of 25% of employees either department-wide or by work unit
    Transfer of cross-trained employees to back fill staff vacancies
    Allow work-at-home and/or off-site
    Cancel scheduled vacations
    Allow flex-time
    Encourage overtime
Loss of 50% of employees either department-wide or by work unit
    All of the above continue to apply, however, overtime would become mandatory for key
       employees. Please see attached Key Personnel Matrix for back ups.
    Request assistance of additional employees; recall laid off employees, retirees, prior
       employees
    Use of part-time and/or temporary staffing for general clerical responsibilities. Prior to
       our office having a 50% reduction of staffing we will have posted job listings and started
       training programs.
    Re-assignment of cross-trained staff to different departments
    Alert clients and courts of current, local outbreak and reduction in work force
    Communicate with local county offices regarding their current situation and possible
       extension of court appearances in order to decrease the spread of the contagion.

4. Preparation
The Executive Vice President will maintain a list of contacts in the health profession to provide
consultation and advice regarding this plan and its implementation.
The Executive Vice President will, at least annually prior to the influenza season, provide
information to all employees regarding those practices that are recommended by public health
officials that will reduce the spread of the infection. The Executive Vice President will also
develop a list of recommended infection control supplies (hand soaps, tissues, and so on) and
ensure that each location has a sufficient supply of them.
5. Confidentiality of Medical Information
Our policy is to treat any medical information obtained from a disability-related injury or
medical examination, as well as any medical information voluntarily disclosed by an employee,
such as contracting influenza, as a confidential medical record. In furtherance of this policy,
any disclosure or medical information is in limited circumstances with team leaders,
management, human resource director or safety officials as required by law.
6. Testing Our Plan
Management will conduct random drills to test the effectiveness of our plan. Management will
also conduct an annual assessment of the Infectious Disease/Pandemic Plan and modify or
supplement this policy as necessary.
